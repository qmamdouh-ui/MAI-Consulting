Who we talk to

Four people feel this problem. Which of these questions is yours?

Across NGOs, foundations, UN agencies and public institutions of 20 to 500 staff. Find yourself below.

The executive director

Has the board asked for a position on AI, and do you need one before the next meeting?

The job: Answer the board and the funders on AI with a written position.

The problem: A board member asked. A donor questionnaire has an AI section. You suspect staff use it and do not know how.

The result: A scored baseline and a policy in force before the next board meeting, and a written answer for the donor questionnaire.

How you cope today: “We'll look into it.”

How we get there: AI Readiness Assessment →

The data protection officer

Have you found organisational documents in a consumer chatbot, or do you suspect you would?

The job: Prevent the incident, pass the audit, keep the tool list under control.

The problem: You wrote a “don't” memo. Use went underground, and now you cannot see it.

The result: A policy people follow because it permits. An approved tool list, a data classification table, an evidence file.

How you cope today: Prohibition, blocking, hope.

How we get there: AI Use Policy →

The HR or learning lead

Did you buy a learning platform that most staff never finished?

The job: Deliver training people complete and use, and spend the training line defensibly.

The problem: Completion in the teens. You cannot show what changed. Staff still ask what they are allowed to do.

The result: Ninety minutes per department that fits a real calendar. An attendance record and a before-and-after score.

How you cope today: Renew the licence.

How we get there: Team AI Training →

The head of programmes or fundraising

Is every proposal drafted with AI a little differently, by people who never compared notes?

The job: Get proposals and donor reports out without burning the team out.

The problem: Uneven, private AI use. Fear that a junior sends a fabricated figure to a donor.

The result: Countable hours back on the next three proposals. One page the team actually uses. Permission to share what works.

How you cope today: Overtime and personal accounts.

How we get there: Team AI Training and Custom Tools →

Who decides, who pays, who benefits. The head of programmes feels this most and signs least. The executive director decides and usually pays; the data protection officer decides on policy and can block or sponsor; HR pays from the training line when the trigger is the training cycle. We talk first to the director or the DPO, deliver for programmes, and give HR the evidence it needs.

Training centres and academies work with us as delivery partners. Private-sector work is taken on referral. Organisations under 20 staff are usually better served by a free course, and we will say so.

Foundations and donors are a separate conversation. If you fund organisations rather than run one, the work is a portfolio programme: one assessment across the cohort, a shared policy each grantee adapts, cohort training, and one report to you. See portfolio programmes →

How we work with you

Four steps, and you own what is left behind.

Step 01

Discovery call

Thirty minutes. What is already happening with AI in your organisation, and which of the five services you actually need, including none of them.

Step 02

Baseline

The assessment, plus interviews where the package includes them. You get a score and a ranked list before anyone is trained.

Step 03

Delivery

Policy drafted together with your people. Sessions built on your teams' real tasks and real documents.

Step 04

Handover

A re-score, the evidence file, and the cheatsheets in your hands. Engagements close with a capability you own.
