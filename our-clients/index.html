Four kinds of client

Different constraints. Same four services.

What changes between them is the data rules, the disclosure obligations and the vocabulary — not the method.

Nonprofits, NGOs and foundations

Twenty to five hundred staff, no in-house AI function, and a board asking questions. Usually the fastest to see results, because proposal and reporting workloads are concentrated and repetitive.

Donor reporting Beneficiary data Safeguarding

UN agencies and international organisations

Multilateral process, procurement rules, multiple working languages and a low tolerance for reputational risk. Governance and evidence usually matter more than speed.

Procurement-ready Multilingual Standards-mapped

Government and public institutions

Public accountability, records obligations and staff who need to know precisely where the line sits. Applied AI training for officials, and policy written to survive scrutiny.

Officials training Records Arabic delivery

Training centres and academies

Not clients — partners. Ready-to-deliver material, a facilitator kit and co-delivery, so AI literacy joins your catalogue without you building it.

Facilitator kit Co-delivery Train-the-trainer

Private-sector work is taken on referral. It is not what this practice is built around.

How we work with you

Four steps, and you own what is left behind.

Discovery call

Thirty minutes. What is already happening with AI in your organisation, and which of the four services you actually need — including none of them.

Step 02

Baseline

The assessment, plus interviews where the package includes them. You get a score and a ranked list before anyone is trained.

Step 03

Delivery

Policy drafted with your people, not for them. Sessions built on your teams' real tasks and real documents.

Handover

A re-score, the evidence file, and the cheatsheets in your hands. Engagements close with a capability you own, not a dependency.
