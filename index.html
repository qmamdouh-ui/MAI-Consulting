Who this is for

Mission-driven and international organisations of up to 500 staff: NGOs, foundations, UN agencies and public institutions, typically without an in-house AI function and with beneficiary, donor or personal data in their daily work. We speak first with the executive director or the data protection officer.

The situation, with sources

Adoption across the sector is already high. The method around it is not.

Where a figure could not be traced to the organisation that published it, it is not on this site. Three that could:

Use is routine and individual

In a benchmark study of 346 nonprofits, 92% report using AI, 81% use it individually without shared workflows, and only 7% report major improvements in organisational capability. source

Few staff have a working grasp of it

Surveying 215 foundations and 451 nonprofits in April 2025, the Center for Effective Philanthropy reports that almost two-thirds say none or just a few of their staff have a solid understanding of AI and its applications. source

The barrier named is training

Among UK charities, 76% now use AI tools, up from 61% a year earlier. Small charities name limited digital skills (48%) and lack of training (44%) as their biggest barriers to adopting them. source

And the gain, where it has been measured, is fast. In a randomised experiment with 453 professionals, writing tasks took 40% less time at 18% higher rated quality source. Across 5,179 support agents, output rose 14%, and 34% for the least experienced staff source. Among 758 consultants, work was 25% faster with 12.2% more tasks completed source. The same consulting study found the reverse on a task chosen to sit outside what the tool does well: 19 percentage points worse than using no AI at all source. Knowing which tasks to hand over is the whole difference, and it takes one session to teach.

The method

One framework underpins every service.

Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence: the AI Fluency framework published by Anthropic with Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller. The framework is public, so the method can be verified against it.

Delegation

What to hand to AI, what to keep human, and who decides.

Description

How to ask, with your organisation's context already in the request.

Discernment

How to check an output before anyone relies on it.

Diligence

What to log, what to disclose, and who owns the result.

They are the four axes of your assessment score, the four sections of every team's playbook, the four clause groups of your policy and the four modules of every session. The diagnosis, the training and the rules use one vocabulary, so a gap found in the assessment maps directly onto a clause and a lab.
