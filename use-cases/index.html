The ladder

Seven levels. Levels 1 and 2 are where people begin; level 3 is the floor.

Days are delivery days from the level below. Percentages are planning assumptions on the tasks in scope, not promises: level 3 sits at the lower edge of the published field studies because verification is added to the work, levels 4 and 5 are extrapolations that each engagement replaces with its own timing, and levels 6 and 7 pay off through other people and through volume. The sources are on the training page.

Why this and not another free course

Curated, minimum sufficient, built on your own tasks, verified.

  • Curated. Each level names what you must understand and what the free content pushes at you that you do not need yet. You read one page, not forty.
  • Minimum sufficient. The background is kept to what your work needs at that level. Prompt frameworks with acronyms, API keys and benchmarks wait until they are useful, which for most people is never.
  • Built on your own tasks. Every exercise in the course is one of your real tasks, on your real documents, inside your organisation's data rules. The playbook you leave with is yours.
  • Verified. Each level has a proof: three real tasks delivered with the checks recorded, a Project a colleague can use without you, a delegated job with a test set, a pipeline a second person has run. You know you are there because something exists that was not there before.

The method follows the four competencies of the AI Fluency framework published by Anthropic with Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller source. The framework is public, so the course can be checked against it.
