**Correction, 14 October 2026.** An earlier version of this entry, dated 8 September, described the Digital Omnibus as a proposal and said no proposal moved Article 4. Both statements were out of date: the Omnibus was already law. This version replaces it.

The most common thing I hear at the moment, usually with visible relief, is that the AI Act
has been pushed back and there is now time.

Part of it was pushed back. The part that applies to most mission-driven organisations was
rewritten instead.

## What the law now says

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, was published in the Official Journal
on 24 July 2026 and is in force. It does two things that matter here.

**It delayed the high-risk obligations.** Chapter III duties for Annex III systems now apply
from 2 December 2027 instead of 2 August 2026, and for Annex I systems from 2 August 2028
instead of 2 August 2027. If you run a recruitment scoring system, biometric identification
or a system that decides access to essential services, that timeline matters to you.

**It replaced Article 4.** Providers and deployers now have to take measures to *support the
development of* AI literacy among their staff and other people using AI on their behalf. The
text adds that this does not require them to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of
any individual. The Commission and Member States are tasked with supporting the effort, and
the AI Board with recommendations.

## What did not move

Article 4 sits in Chapter I. It has applied since 2 February 2025, and the amendment did not
change that date. An organisation using a chatbot to draft donor reports was never in
Chapter III. It was in Chapter I, and it still is. Who is in scope is unchanged: deployers,
and people acting on their behalf.

## What this means for the plan

Two sentences you may have heard are both wrong. "The AI Act was delayed, so we have time"
confuses Chapter III with Chapter I. "Article 4 was watered down, so we can drop it" confuses
a lighter legal standard with the questions you will actually be asked.

The legal duty is now about effort and proportion: what did you do, and was it sensible for
the people and uses involved. A policy, a tool inventory, role-based training and an
attendance record are still the plainest way to answer that. The people who ask are a funder
writing a due-diligence questionnaire, a board member, an insurer. None of them are waiting
for the Official Journal.

Switzerland is outside the EU. A Geneva organisation is reached by the Act through its
EU-facing activity, contracts and funding, which is a question to put to your own counsel.

## The practical takeaway

Check which chapter the person who said "we have time" was talking about. Then search your
own documents for "sufficient level of AI literacy": anything quoting it cites superseded text.
