Why this works quickly

Controlled studies keep finding the same thing: the gain is large, it arrives fast, and it is largest for the people who were struggling.

Published field experiments, not our results. They matter because they isolate the variable that decides whether an organisation gets the gain at all: which tasks are handed over, and how the output is checked.

40%

less time on professional writing tasks, with output quality rated 18% higher, in a randomised experiment with 453 college-educated professionals. Science, 2023

14%

more issues resolved per hour across 5,179 customer support agents, rising to 34% for the least experienced staff. NBER, 2023

25%

faster on 18 realistic knowledge-work tasks, with 12.2% more tasks completed at higher quality, among 758 consultants. HBS working paper, 2023

The same study found the opposite effect on the wrong task. On one task chosen to sit outside what the tool does well, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct answer than colleagues using none. HBS working paper, 2023 That single finding is the argument for training: the difference between a large gain and a costly error is knowing which tasks to hand over and how to check what comes back. It takes one session to teach and it is the whole of what we teach first.

We do not publish a return-on-investment percentage for our own engagements. We have not measured enough of them to state one, and a figure quoted before your documents have been seen has no basis. What we do instead is time the tasks on each department's page before the lab and ninety days after, so the number you end up with is your own.

The situation, with sources

Adoption is already high. The method around it is not.

Use is routine and individual

92% of nonprofits report using AI, 81% use it individually without shared workflows, and only 7% report major improvements in organisational capability. 2026 benchmark of 346 nonprofits

Few staff have a working grasp of it

Across 215 foundations and 451 nonprofits, almost two-thirds report that none or just a few of their staff have a solid understanding of AI and its applications. CEP, 2025

The barrier named is training

Among UK charities, 76% now use AI tools, up from 61% a year earlier. Small charities name limited digital skills (48%) and lack of training (44%) as their biggest barriers. Charity Digital Skills Report 2025

The method

One framework underpins all five.

Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence: the AI Fluency framework published by Anthropic with Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller. source It is public, so the method can be checked against it.

Delegation

What to hand to AI, what to keep human, and who decides.

Description

How to ask, with your organisation's context already in the request.

Discernment

How to check an output before anyone relies on it.

Diligence

What to log, what to disclose, and who owns the result.

The same four words are the axes of your score, the sections of every playbook, the clause groups of your policy and the modules of every session, so a gap found in the assessment maps directly onto a policy clause and a training lab.

What this practice does not do

No model fine-tuning, no data pipelines, no custom software. We configure agents, skills and tools on platforms you already use, and stop there.

We do not offer regulatory compliance services, certification or legal advice. Our documents use the vocabulary of recognised frameworks so that they are legible to your counterparts, and where a legal or compliance opinion is needed we will say so rather than provide it.
