The problem, stated so you can check it

Your programme, fundraising and operations staff are already drafting donor reports with consumer AI tools — this week, with beneficiary data in hand.

There are no rules that permit it, no training built for their actual role, and no answer when a funder asks. That is the whole problem. Here is the evidence, and what it costs.

It happens weekly, not annually

Every proposal, every donor report, every board paper. 76% of UK charities now use AI tools, up from 61% a year earlier. 92% of US nonprofits report some use — 81% of it ad hoc and undocumented.

Charity Digital Skills Report 2025 (672 charities); Nonprofit AI Adoption Report 2026 (346 nonprofits).

What it costs

Only 7% of nonprofits report major impact from AI. 88% of professionals have put work information into public chatbots, a third of it customer or beneficiary data. And when a funder's due-diligence pack asks about AI governance, an organisation with no policy has nothing to send.

Shadow-AI surveys 2026; CEP AI With Purpose, 2025.

How organisations cope today

They tolerate it. Or buy a catalogue licence after which over 90% of staff still feel unprepared. Or IT circulates a “don't” memo and use goes underground. 47% still have no AI governance policy — and among those without one, the barriers named are training (48%) and not knowing where to start (44%).

The current alternative is the real competitor. We put it in the comparison table.

How the four fit together

Four words run through everything we give you.

Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence — the AI Fluency framework published by Anthropic with Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller. It is public, so you can check our work.

Delegation

What to hand to AI, what to keep human, and who decides.

Description

How to ask, with your organisation's context already in the request.

Discernment

How to check an output before anyone relies on it.

Diligence

What to log, what to disclose, and who owns the result.

These are the four axes of your assessment score, the four sections of every team's cheatsheet, the four clause groups of your policy, and the four modules of every session. The obvious question

“Why pay you when there are free options?”

You should use them. Free introductory courses on this framework exist, published by the people who wrote it. Subsidised cohort programmes exist for smaller nonprofits. If one of those is the right first step for you, we will say so on the call.

Then come to us for the four things a course or a cohort structurally cannot do: write your policy, use your team's actual tasks and documents, clear your own data rules, and produce the evidence a regulator or a donor asks for. A curriculum taught to two hundred organisations at once cannot tell your finance officer which tool is approved for which data, in your organisation.

We have put every alternative side by side with its published price, including the ones that are free and the ones that would exclude you on eligibility. Compare the market →

If you operate in the EU. Since 2 February 2025, Article 4 of the EU AI Act has required organisations deploying AI to ensure that staff — and contractors acting on their behalf — have AI literacy proportionate to their role, context and risk. It is outcome-based: you have to be able to demonstrate it. Department-specific training, a documented policy and an attendance record are what that demonstration looks like.
